# Research Pass 007 — Reciprocal Protoss greeting in official fiction

**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**Scope:** Official StarCraft literary evidence for pragmatic function and cross-community use of `En taro Adun`.

## Evidence A — *Children of the Void*

Official Blizzard-hosted story: https://media.blizzard.com/sc2/lore/children-void/children-void.pdf

Searchable PDF text at **printed p. 12** includes a brief exchange where Vorazun greets Mohandar with `En taro Adun` and Mohandar reciprocates. Both are Nerazim. This is direct narrative evidence for formulaic salutation/response usage.

## Evidence B — *Cold Symmetry*

Official Blizzard-hosted story: https://media.blizzard.com/sc2/lore/cold-symmetry/cold-symmetry.pdf

Searchable PDF text at **printed p. 36** shows a fleet communication addressed to a Protoss zealot (Teredal), including `En taro Adun`, and Teredal subsequently repeating the same formula. This extends the documented social context beyond a pair of Nerazim.

**Status:** Publisher literary text extract corroborated; direct page screenshot failed and remains pending. The fleet voice is not identified with enough certainty to assign its faction.

## Supported linguistic analysis

- The full `En taro Adun` formula is **reciprocal** in two narrative contexts.
- In one scene the speakers are both Nerazim; in the other an Aiur Protoss zealot participates.
- This supports **pragmatic usage as greeting or respectful acknowledgment**, but does **not** reveal segment meanings.
- The stories provide no direct English translation or phonetic transcription.
- Capitalization is source-specific and should remain unchanged: `En taro Adun` rather than silently capitalized `En Taro Adun`.
- A shared formula does **not** entail identical phonology or a single uniform dialect across factions.

## Research consequence

Create a preliminary hypothesis **NK-HYP-001**: `En taro [honored name]` may constitute a ceremonial formula. Current literary examples confirm only the fixed `En taro Adun` string, **not** productive substitution. Test the pattern using independent primary attestations of `En taro Tassadar` and `En taro Zeratul`, controlling for authorship and chronology.
