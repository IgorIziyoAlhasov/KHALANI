# Research Pass 009 — Testing *En Taro* with Zeratul and Artanis

**Date:** 2026-11-26  
**Scope:** Targeted search for the `En Taro Zeratul` formula and additional name substitutions.

## Finding A — A highly specific in-game scene lead

The StarCraft Wiki's *Legacy of the Void* **Return to Aiur missions** dialogue transcript attributes the following three consecutive calls to Artanis:

- `En Taro Adun!`
- `En Taro Tassadar!`
- `En Taro Zeratul!`

It then attributes `En Taro Artanis!` to Selendis.

Source: https://starcraft.fandom.com/wiki/StarCraft_II:_Legacy_of_the_Void_campaign_quotations/Return_to_Aiur_missions

This is a **secondary transcript of a purported primary cutscene**. It supplies a concrete sequence and speaker attributions for subsequent original-game verification. It is **not** yet the original audio, subtitle file, or raw game text.

## Finding B — A separate reciprocal exchange

The *Aiur missions* transcript records Artanis addressing Zeratul with `En Taro Tassadar, old friend.` and Zeratul responding `En Taro Artanis, brother.`

Source: https://starcraft.fandom.com/wiki/StarCraft_II:_Legacy_of_the_Void_campaign_quotations/Aiur_missions

In context this supports the **hypothesis** that the fixed preamble can introduce an honored name in formal address, and is not restricted to deceased honorees. The latter interpretation remains tentative because the transcript is secondary and no grammar is specified.

## Methodological interpretation

- **Publisher-verified written fillers to date:** Adun, Tassadar.
- **New secondary transcript fillers:** Zeratul, Artanis.
- **Strong clue to a formula:** three sequential same-speaker utterances with only the name varying.
- **Still unknown:** whether `en` / `taro` are independent morphemes, what the construction exactly means, and whether any name is grammatically allowed.
- **Source caution:** the StarCraft Wiki transcript represents the game rather than an official game-export file. Never promote these new lines to primary-verified until direct asset inspection.

A Spanish-dubbed Artanis voice-line video *description* reportedly includes `En Taro, Zeratul`; the video itself has not been analyzed. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-BYxrzUA96U . This is merely an additional localized transcription lead.
